1. What Flipside is

Flipside reads food labels and gives each product a score out of 100, with the reasoning behind it.

The score is an opinion, not a fact about your health. It is calculated from public nutrition data and the ingredient list using a published method — you can read the whole method in the app, under How we score. Reasonable people, and other apps, will disagree with it.

It is not medical or nutritional advice. It does not know your medical history, your medications, your blood work, or what you have eaten today. It cannot tell you whether a food is safe or good for you specifically. If you have a health condition, an eating disorder, an allergy, or you are pregnant, decide with a qualified professional — not with a number in an app.

Do not rely on it for allergies. Flipside can flag allergens you ask it to watch for, and that flag can be wrong: the underlying record may be incomplete, out of date, or refer to a different formulation of the product. Manufacturers also change recipes without changing barcodes. Always read the packet yourself. A missed allergen can be serious, and the app is not a substitute for the label in your hand.

2. Where the information comes from

Product information comes from Open Food Facts, an open database maintained by volunteers, and from USDA FoodData Central. Some of it comes from labels photographed by other people using the app.

None of those sources is guaranteed to be accurate, complete, or current. We check what we can — a shared reading is only trusted once two independent readings agree — and we still cannot promise a record matches the packet in front of you.

If a product looks wrong, tap "This looks wrong". That is the fastest way to get it corrected for everybody.
